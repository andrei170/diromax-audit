Your website
The good stuff is there, but hidden
  • No tap-to-call on any page we read, and the phone numbers are only in the footer.
  • No reviews or star rating shown, just a link to Google.
  • The only form is on the contact page, with 8 required fields.
  • On a phone, the main heading runs off the screen and there is no call or quote button on the first screen.

Your Google listing
Great reviews, wrong place on the map

The listing shows 5.0 from 61 reviews and 01248 305 007. Google puts its pin in the hills north-west of Bala, about 40 miles from Cemaes Bay. In three Maps searches it came 12th, 13th and outside the first 13, behind firms with a fraction of the reviews.

Ads
Meta (Facebook and Instagram): one ad, now stopped
  • Ran: 28 November 2025 to 24 January 2026, about 8 weeks, on Facebook, Instagram and Messenger. Two copies of one ad. Nothing is running today.
  • Creative: a phone video of a gutter being cleared, headed “Are Your Gutters Ready for the Rain?”
  • Offer: “FREE Inspection, No commitment”, then “Tap Message to get your FREE inspection & personalised price”.
  • Button: Send message (Messenger).
What was good about it
  • A real video of your own work, which is what people trust.
  • A seasonal hook at the right time of year: gutters before the winter rain.
  • Messaging is an easy first step for someone on a phone.
What held it back
  • No price, when you already publish one. “Gutters cleared from £50” tells people what to expect and filters out the ones who were never going to book.
  • A free inspection for a £50 job means a trip out before any money is made. A photo sent by WhatsApp does the same job without the drive.
  • Messages ask nothing. No postcode, no job type, so every chat starts from zero, by hand, while you are on a job.
  • No tracking on the website, so nobody can say which messages became paid jobs.
  • One ad, one service, eight weeks, then off. Exterior cleaning sells all year if the service changes with the season.
  • No repeat offer. Every customer from the ad was a one-off.
How we would run it instead
  • Always on, small, and seasonal: gutters in autumn and winter, roofs and render in spring, driveways and patios in summer.
  • Price in the ad: “from £50”, with your own before and after video.
  • A short form inside Facebook: service, postcode, mobile and an optional photo, then an automatic text back.
  • Google search ads for “gutter cleaning Anglesey”-type searches, which you have never run.
  • A plan offer on every job: the first clean is the ad's job; getting them back every 6 or 12 months is the follow-up's job.

The Google Ads Transparency Centre shows no ads, at any time, in the UK for diromaxexteriorcleaning.co.uk. Nobody searching “gutter cleaning Anglesey” sees a Diromax ad.

SEO and AI answers
What we tested
  • Google Maps (20 October 2026): 12th, 13th and not in the first 13 for three everyday searches (see Competitors).
  • AI answers: an AI web-search assistant named Diromax for gutter and roof cleaning near Cemaes Bay, then said it has zero reviews, quoting FreeIndex. It did not see the 61 on Google, because nothing on your own site says so.
  • Your site: one page for eight services, no town pages, no local-business data for Google, reviews not shown, and 73 blog posts that stopped on 30 May 2025.
What we would do
  • Fix the Google listing first: service area set to the towns you actually want, so the map position sits on Anglesey, not near Bala. Add the mobile, services with prices, and a photo every week.
  • A page for each service and each main town, so “roof cleaning Bangor” has a page to land on.
  • Show the 61 reviews on the site, with the rating marked up for Google and for AI tools to read.
  • Correct FreeIndex and the other directories so they say the same thing.
  • Fewer, better blog posts: answers to real questions, like “how often should gutters be cleaned on Anglesey?”, which is what AI answers quote.
